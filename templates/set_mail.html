What your domain needs or the mail sends and lands in spam

A relay will happily accept a message for a domain that has not authorised it. The receiving side is what rejects it. Three records on the domain in your from address settle that, and all three are free:

RecordName ValueWhy
SPF
TXT
 @ v=spf1 include:your-relay ~all Says this relay may send as you. Your provider gives you the include to use. One SPF record only — a second one breaks both.
DKIM
TXT or CNAME
 selector._domainkey generated by your provider — copy it exactly Signs each message so it survives being forwarded, which SPF alone does not.
DMARC
TXT
 _dmarc v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@example.com Start at p=none and read the reports before tightening it. Going straight to reject is how a company stops receiving its own invoices.
A from address on a domain you do not control will be rejected or silently binned however correct the relay settings are. And sending is separate from receiving: if nothing is listening on that address, bounces and replies to your invitations go nowhere — worth pointing the from address somewhere real, or setting a reply-to that is.
